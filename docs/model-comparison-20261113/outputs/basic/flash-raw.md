# Case 2

This observational cohort analysis of 220,089 participants identified 8,459 incident breast cancer cases (3.84%) over a median follow-up of 11.6 years. Sleep scores of 0-1, compared with a score of 5, were associated with a higher risk of breast cancer (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11-1.52). The metabolic signature was also associated with risk (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.32-1.99), and it mediated 13.5% of the observed association between sleep pattern and breast cancer risk. These findings describe associations and do not establish that sleep pattern or the metabolic signature causes breast cancer. The most important limitation is the observational cohort design, which permits an assessment of association and precludes causal inference. The mediation estimate should be interpreted within that design. The results are therefore hypothesis-generating and require cautious interpretation, with further study needed before they support preventive or clinical action based on sleep pattern modification.

# Case 3

In this retrospective single-centre series of 32 consecutive patients, overall staging concordance was 53.1%. Upstaging occurred in 21.9% and downstaging in 25.0%. For assessment of myometrial invasion, sensitivity was 58.3%, specificity 65.0%, positive predictive value 50.0%, and negative predictive value 72.2%. These estimates indicate that staging agreement was limited and that both overstaging and understaging occurred. The small sample of 32 patients and the retrospective, single-centre design make the estimates imprecise and limit their generalisability to other clinical populations. The findings should therefore be interpreted as exploratory and require confirmation in larger, more representative studies before they can inform a change in practice.

Corrected English version:

This single-centre retrospective cohort enrolled 30 patients with DAT-negative AIHA, accounting for 4.77% of contemporaneous AIHA admissions. Female patients comprised 20/30 (66.7%) and male patients 10/30 (33.3%); primary disease accounted for 26/30 (86.7%) and secondary disease for 4/30 (13.3%). First-line glucocorticoids achieved an overall response in 28/30 patients (93.3%). After a median follow-up of 64 months, all-cause mortality was 3/30 (10.0%), and one-, three-, and five-year event-free survival rates were 56.5%, 45.9%, and 37.5%, respectively. These findings describe outcomes in this small retrospective cohort; they do not establish durability, cure, or a standard treatment for this subtype.
