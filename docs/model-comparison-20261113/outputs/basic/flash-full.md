# Case 1

The model was developed and internally validated in 1,702 patients in Switzerland, among whom the internally validated AUC was 0.72 (95% CI 0.63 to 0.81). External validation included 851 patients in the United Kingdom and yielded an AUC of 0.64 (95% CI 0.58 to 0.70). Both estimates indicate discrimination, although the lower external AUC suggests that performance may differ across populations. Calibration was assessed, but the supplied findings do not establish decision impact, workflow effects, or patient benefit. These results support the model as a promising candidate for further clinical evaluation; without decision-curve, workflow-impact, or randomized clinical-utility evidence, however, they do not establish that using the model improves clinical decisions or outcomes. Clinical application therefore remains potential rather than established.

# Case 2

In this observational cohort of 220,089 participants, 8,459 incident breast cancer cases occurred (3.84%) over a median follow-up of 11.6 years. Compared with a sleep score of 5, a score of 0-1 was associated with a higher hazard of breast cancer (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.52). The metabolic signature was also associated with the outcome (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.32 to 1.99) and mediated 13.5% of the observed association. Because the study was observational, it cannot establish causality, and residual confounding may affect both the sleep-cancer association and the estimated mediation. These findings support an association between lower sleep scores and breast cancer risk that was partly related to the metabolic signature, while intervention effects and clinical benefit remain unestablished.

# Case 3

In this retrospective single-centre study of 32 consecutive patients, preoperative MRI showed overall endometrial carcinoma staging concordance of 53.1%; upstaging occurred in 21.9% and downstaging in 25.0%. For myometrial invasion, sensitivity was 58.3%, specificity 65.0%, positive predictive value 50.0%, and negative predictive value 72.2%.

The small sample and retrospective single-centre design limit the precision and generalizability of these diagnostic estimates, so the findings do not support a change in clinical practice.

**Corrected English version**

This single-centre retrospective cohort included 30 patients with direct antiglobulin test (DAT)-negative autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (AIHA), accounting for 4.77% of contemporaneous AIHA admissions. There were 20/30 female patients (66.7%) and 10/30 male patients (33.3%); primary disease occurred in 26/30 patients (86.7%) and secondary disease in 4/30 (13.3%). The overall response to first-line glucocorticoids was 93.3% (28/30). During a median follow-up of 64 months, all-cause mortality was 10.0% (3/30). The 1-, 3-, and 5-year event-free survival (EFS) rates were 56.5%, 45.9%, and 37.5%, respectively. These findings describe the clinical features and treatment outcomes of a small single-centre retrospective cohort; they do not establish durable response, cure, or general use of glucocorticoids as standard treatment.
