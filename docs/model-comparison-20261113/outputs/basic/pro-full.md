# Case 1

In internal validation, the model showed an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.72 (95% CI 0.63 to 0.81). This result supports moderate discrimination within the development cohort. In external validation, the AUC was 0.64 (95% CI 0.58 to 0.70), which supports the conclusion that the model's discrimination was lower when it was tested in a new population. Calibration was assessed, but the abstract reports no decision-curve, workflow-impact, or randomized clinical-utility study. These results therefore support limited generalizability rather than readiness for routine use, and they do not establish that applying the model would improve clinical decisions or patient outcomes. The model may still be regarded as having potential for clinical application, but its clinical utility remains unproven and requires further evaluation.

# Case 2

In this observational cohort of 220,089 participants followed for a median of 11.6 years, 8,459 incident breast cancer cases were recorded (3.84%). A sleep score of 0 to 1 was associated with a higher risk of breast cancer than a sleep score of 5 (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.52), and the metabolic signature was associated with a higher risk (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.32 to 1.99). The metabolic signature mediated 13.5% of the observed association. These findings indicate an association between poorer sleep patterns and breast cancer risk, with part of that association potentially operating through the metabolic signature. Because this was an observational analysis, unmeasured confounding may explain some or all of the observed associations, and the results do not establish a causal relationship.

# Case 3

Among 32 consecutive patients who underwent preoperative MRI for endometrial carcinoma staging, overall staging concordance was 53.1%. MRI upstaged 21.9% of patients and downstaged 25.0%. For myometrial invasion, sensitivity was 58.3%, specificity was 65.0%, the positive predictive value was 50.0%, and the negative predictive value was 72.2%. These estimates derive from a retrospective single-centre series of 32 consecutive patients, so their precision and generalizability are limited.

These findings do not support a change in clinical practice.

# Case 5

**Mismatches**

1. Sample size: the abstract states 32 patients; the source cohort has 30 patients.
2. Denominator: the abstract says 4.77% of contemporaneous admissions; the source specifies 4.77% of contemporaneous AIHA admissions.
3. Endpoint and result: the abstract reports a five-year event-free survival rate of 45.9%; in the source, 45.9% is the three-year rate, and the five-year rate is 37.5%. The abstract also omits the one-year rate of 56.5%.
4. Claim strength: "all responses were durable" is not reported; the source does not state response durability, and EFS declines from 56.5% at one year to 37.5% at five years.
5. Claim strength: "glucocorticoids cure most patients" overstates the source, which reports an overall response rate of 93.3% (28/30), not cure.
6. Claim strength: "should be the standard treatment for this subtype" is a treatment recommendation not supported by a single-centre retrospective cohort of 30 patients.
7. Completeness: the abstract omits the male proportion (10/30, 33.3%) and the primary/secondary disease distribution (26/30, 86.7% and 4/30, 13.3%).

**Corrected English version**

This single-centre retrospective cohort study enrolled 30 patients with direct antiglobulin test-negative autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (DAT-negative AIHA), accounting for 4.77% of contemporaneous AIHA admissions. Twenty patients (66.7%) were female and 10 (33.3%) were male; primary disease was present in 26 patients (86.7%) and secondary disease in 4 patients (13.3%). First-line glucocorticoid treatment achieved an overall response in 93.3% of patients (28/30). After a median follow-up of 64 months, all-cause mortality was 10.0% (3/30). The one-, three-, and five-year event-free survival rates were 56.5%, 45.9%, and 37.5%, respectively. In this cohort, first-line glucocorticoids achieved a high overall response rate, but the declining event-free survival and the small single-centre retrospective design do not establish response durability, cure, or that glucocorticoids should be the standard treatment for this subtype.
