# Case 2

In this observational cohort of 220,089 UK Biobank participants, 8,459 incident breast cancers (3.84%) occurred during a median follow-up of 11.6 years. A lower sleep score (0-1 versus 5) was associated with a higher risk of breast cancer (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.52). A higher metabolic signature was also associated with elevated risk (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.32 to 1.99), and this signature mediated 13.5% of the observed association. These estimates are consistent with a modest link between sleep patterns, metabolic markers, and breast cancer risk, but the observational design limits causal interpretation. Residual confounding, reverse causation, and measurement error could influence the associations, and the mediated proportion should be interpreted as an estimate rather than a proven mechanism.

# Case 3

Among 32 consecutive patients undergoing preoperative MRI, overall staging concordance was 53.1%. MRI upstaged disease in 21.9% and downstaged disease in 25.0%. For myometrial invasion, sensitivity was 58.3%, specificity 65.0%, positive predictive value 50.0%, and negative predictive value 72.2%. These estimates are based on a small, retrospective, single-centre sample, so they should be interpreted with caution and may not be generalizable. The available evidence does not support changing clinical practice based on these findings.

Corrected English version:

This single-centre retrospective cohort included 30 patients with DAT-negative autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, accounting for 4.77% of contemporaneous AIHA admissions. Twenty patients were female (66.7%) and 10 were male (33.3%); 26 patients (86.7%) had primary disease and 4 (13.3%) had secondary disease. First-line glucocorticoids achieved an overall response in 93.3% of patients (28/30). After a median follow-up of 64 months, all-cause mortality was 10.0% (3/30). The one-, three-, and five-year event-free survival rates were 56.5%, 45.9%, and 37.5%, respectively. These findings suggest that first-line glucocorticoids are associated with a high initial response in this small retrospective cohort, but event-free survival declined over time and the evidence does not establish that glucocorticoids cure patients or should be the standard treatment.
