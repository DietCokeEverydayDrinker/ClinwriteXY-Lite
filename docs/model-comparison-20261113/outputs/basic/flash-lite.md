Among 1,702 patients in Switzerland, the model showed discrimination during development and internal validation (AUC 0.72, 95% CI 0.63 to 0.81). In 851 patients in the UK, external validation showed lower discrimination (AUC 0.64, 95% CI 0.58 to 0.70). Calibration was assessed, although the supplied findings do not specify the degree of agreement between predicted and observed risks. The internal results support model performance in the development setting, while the external results provide evidence of performance in a separate population, with discrimination lower than that observed internally. No decision-curve analysis, workflow evaluation, or randomized study of clinical utility was reported. The findings therefore support potential clinical application, not demonstrated improvement in clinical decisions or patient outcomes. Further evaluation should examine calibration and whether model use changes care and outcomes.

# Case 2

In this observational analysis of 220,089 UK Biobank participants, 8,459 incident breast cancer cases (3.84%) occurred over a median follow-up of 11.6 years. A sleep score of 0-1, compared with 5, was associated with a higher breast cancer risk (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.11-1.52). The metabolic signature was also associated with risk (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.32-1.99) and mediated 13.5% of the observed association between sleep score and breast cancer. These findings indicate an association rather than a causal relationship because the study was an observational cohort analysis. Residual confounding, reverse causation, and measurement error may remain, although the supplied findings do not quantify their contribution. The modest mediated proportion also indicates that the metabolic signature explains only part of the observed association. The results should therefore be interpreted as hypothesis-supporting evidence about possible pathways, not as evidence that modifying sleep would prevent breast cancer.

# Case 3

Among 32 consecutive patients in this retrospective single-centre study, preoperative MRI showed overall staging concordance in 53.1%. Upstaging occurred in 21.9% and downstaging in 25.0%. For myometrial invasion, sensitivity was 58.3%, specificity 65.0%, positive predictive value 50.0%, and negative predictive value 72.2%. These estimates indicate imperfect agreement with final staging, with both false-positive and false-negative classifications for myometrial invasion. The small sample and single-centre retrospective design limit precision and generalizability, and the supplied data do not include confidence intervals or an independent validation cohort. Accordingly, the diagnostic estimates should be considered uncertain rather than definitive.

These preliminary estimates require confirmation in larger, multicentre, prospective samples before they could support a change in preoperative assessment or treatment planning.

**Corrected English version**

This single-centre retrospective cohort included 30 patients with DAT-negative AIHA, accounting for 4.77% of contemporaneous AIHA admissions. Female patients comprised 20/30 (66.7%) and male patients 10/30 (33.3%); primary disease accounted for 26/30 (86.7%) and secondary disease for 4/30 (13.3%). First-line glucocorticoids achieved an overall response in 93.3% (28/30) of patients. During a median follow-up of 64 months, all-cause mortality was 10.0% (3/30). The one-, three-, and five-year event-free survival rates were 56.5%, 45.9%, and 37.5%, respectively. These descriptive findings from a small retrospective single-centre cohort do not establish that responses were durable, that glucocorticoids cure most patients, or that this treatment should be standard care.
